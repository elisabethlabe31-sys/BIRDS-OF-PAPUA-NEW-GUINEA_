Birds of Papua New Guinea

Papua New Guinea (PNG) hosts extraordinary bird diversity — over 700 species, many endemic. Below are species highlights (alphabetical) with where to find them, habitat, diet, conservation status, and a fun fact.

IUCN Conservation Status (quick guide)

  • Least Concern: species are widespread or abundant and face no immediate major threats.
  • Near Threatened: species close to qualifying for a threatened category or likely to in the near future.
  • Vulnerable: species facing a high risk of extinction in the wild without conservation actions.

Species Groups & Brief Explanation

PNG's bird fauna includes several important groups: birds-of-paradise (renowned for male courtship displays and sexual dimorphism), parrots and cockatoos (colorful, often noisy, important seed and pollination agents), pigeons and doves (key seed dispersers), kingfishers and raptors (predators), bowerbirds and riflebirds (interesting courtship behaviour), and many small passerines that occupy understory and canopy niches. Mountain uplift and island isolation drove speciation, producing many endemic lineages.

Species & Where to Find Them (alphabetical)

Australasian Figbird (Sphecotheres vieilloti)Least Concern
Where: Lowland forests, edges, and gardens across PNG (provinces such as Milne Bay, Central, Oro, Morobe, Madang, East Sepik, Sandaun). Habitat: canopy and edge. Diet: primarily figs and other soft fruits, supplemented by small insects, nectar, and small seeds. Origin: Native to northern and eastern Australia, southern New Guinea, and Kai Islands.
Fun fact: They often gather in noisy fruiting trees and help disperse fig seeds.
Black Sicklebill (Epimachus fastuosus)Least Concern
Where: Montane forests in the central highlands (provinces such as Simbu, Eastern Highlands, Western Highlands). Habitat: canopy and forest edges. Diet: primarily berries and figs, supplemented by insects and spiders picked from leaves. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Males have long curved tail feathers used in dramatic aerial displays.
Blyth's Hornbill (Rhyticeros plicatus)Near Threatened
Where: Widespread in lowland and hill forests, including many islands (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang, East Sepik, West New Britain, East New Britain, New Ireland). Habitat: canopy and large trees. Diet: mainly fruit; occasional small vertebrates. Origin: Native to Southeast Asia and New Guinea.
Fun fact: Hornbills are important seed dispersers and often nest in tree hollows.
Blue-eyed Cockatoo (Cacatua ophthalmica)Vulnerable
Where: Lowland and hill forests of northern PNG (provinces such as Madang, East Sepik, Sandaun). Habitat: canopy. Diet: cracks open hard nuts and seeds with powerful beak; also consumes fruit, berries, and blossoms. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Named for its pale blue eye-ring — a distinctive field mark.
Brown Cuckoo-Dove (Macropygia amboinensis)Least Concern
Where: Lowland and secondary forests (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: canopy. Diet: almost exclusively frugivorous, swallowing small berries and figs whole. Origin: Native to Southeast Asia and New Guinea.
Fun fact: Often found in pairs or small groups, quietly feeding on fig trees.
Carola's Parotia (Parotia carolae)Near Threatened
Where: Mid-montane forests of central ranges (provinces such as Eastern Highlands, Simbu). Habitat: understory and display grounds. Diet: fruits and arthropods. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Males perform a ballet-like courtship with shiny breast feathers that flare to catch light.
Eclectus Parrot (Eclectus roratus)Least Concern
Where: Coastal lowlands, gardens and disturbed areas (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang, East Sepik). Habitat: canopy and forest edges. Diet: feeds on fruit, flower buds, nectar, and seeds; known to chew on coconuts. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea and surrounding islands.
Fun fact: Sexually dimorphic — males are green and females are red with blue cheeks.
Emperor Bird-of-Paradise (Paradisaea guilielmi)Near Threatened
Where: Hill and lower montane forests in parts of PNG (provinces such as Central, Oro). Habitat: canopy and mid-levels. Diet: frugivorous diet of figs and palm fruit; also picks insects from leaves. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Males have long flank plumes used in display, making them very showy.
Golden Monarch (Carterornis chrysomela)Least Concern
Where: Lowland rainforests and secondary growth (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: canopy and forest edges. Diet: specializes on airborne insects which it sallies to catch; also gleans from leaves. Origin: Native to New Guinea and Australia.
Fun fact: Small vivid yellow bird with black wings and tail, often seen on exposed sunlit branches sallying for insects.
Greater Bird-of-Paradise (Paradisaea apoda)Near Threatened
Where: Lowland and hill rainforests, southern PNG (provinces such as Gulf, Western, Central). Habitat: canopy and display perches. Diet: feeds mainly on figs and other fruits, supplements with insects and small vertebrates. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Historically prized for their long flank plumes used in ceremonial dress.
Hooded PitohuiLeast Concern
Fun fact: Contains batrachotoxin in its skin and feathers — a rare toxic bird.
Hooded Pitta (Pitta sordida)Near Threatened
Where: Dense lowland forest and leaf litter (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe). Habitat: forest floor. Diet: omvertebrates. Origin: Native to Southeast Asia and New Guineall vertebrates, and figs. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Known for its vivid green body and black-and-red head colouring.
King of Saxony Bird-of-Paradise (Pteridophora alberti)Near Threatened
Where: Hill and montane forests of central ranges (provinces such as Eastern Highlands, Simbu). Habitat: mid-canopy. Diet: fruits and small invertebrates. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Males have two extraordinary head wires tipped with flags used in display.
Lesser Bird-of-Paradise (Paradisaea minor)Least Concern
Where: Hill and lower montane forests (provinces such as Eastern Highlands, Simbu, Western Highlands). Habitat: canopy. Diet: fruit and arthropods. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Males perform elaborate dances on display perches to attract females.
Magnificent Riflebird (Ptiloris magnificus)Least Concern
Where: Lowland and hill rainforests (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: understory and mid-canopy. Diet: insects and small frogs. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Males display with a unique wing-flicking motion and shiny plumage flash.
New Guinea Friarbird (Philemon novaeguineae)Least Concern
Where: Widespread in lowlands and disturbed areas (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang, East Sepik). Habitat: canopy and edges. Diet: nectar, fruit and insects. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Friarbirds have a bare patch of skin on the face and a loud, raucous call.
New Guinea Kingfisher (various species)Least Concern
Where: Rivers, mangroves and coastal forests (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang, East Sepik, Sandaun). Habitat: riparian zones. Diet: fish, crustaceans and insects. Origin: Native to New Guinea and surrounding islands.
Fun fact: Many kingfishers in PNG have bright colours and are easier to spot than one might expect.
Palm Cockatoo (Probosciger aterrimus)Near Threatened
Where: Lowland and hill forests, often near coasts (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang, East Sepik, Sandaun). Habitat: canopy. Diet: nuts, seeds and fruit. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea and surrounding islands.
Fun fact: Males use sticks to drum on hollow trees during courtship displays.
Paradise-crow (Lycocorax pyrrhopterus)Least Concern
Where: Lowland forests on some islands and parts of PNG (provinces such as Manus, New Ireland). Habitat: canopy. Diet: fruit and insects. Origin: Endemic to Bismarck Archipelago and New Guinea.
Fun fact: Despite its name, it's more crow-like and lacks the flamboyance of birds-of-paradise.
Pheasant Pigeon (Otidiphaps nobilis)Near Threatened
Where: Forest floor in lowland and lower montane forests (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: leaf litter and understorey. Diet: seeds, fruit, and invertebrates. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: These ground-dwelling pigeons are shy and often only revealed by their deep calls.
Raggiana Bird-of-Paradise (Paradisaea raggiana)Least Concern
Where: Lowland rainforests — particularly southeastern PNG (provinces such as Milne Bay, Central, Oro). Habitat: canopy and forest edges. Diet: fruits and arthropods. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: The national bird of Papua New Guinea — its plumes are used in cultural dress.
Rainbow Lorikeet (Trichoglossus moluccanus)Least Concern
Where: Coastal forests, gardens and floral-rich areas (provinces such as Milne Bay, Central, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: canopy and flowering trees. Diet: nectar and soft fruits. Origin: Native to Australia, New Guinea, and surrounding islands.
Fun fact: Lorikeets have brush-tipped tongues adapted to feed on nectar.
Regent Bowerbird (Sericulus chrysocephalus)Least Concern
Where: Rainforest and adjacent habitats (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: canopy and display courts. Diet: fruit and insects. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Males build decorated bowers to attract females.
Southern Cassowary (Casuarius casuarius)Vulnerable
Where: Lowland rainforests and swampy coastal forests (provinces such as Central, Gulf, Western, Milne Bay). Habitat: forest floor. Diet: large fruits and small animals. Origin: Native to New Guinea and northeastern Australia.
Fun fact: Cassowaries are one of the few birds that disperse very large seeds whole.
Spangled Drongo (Dicrurus bracteatus)Least Concern
Where: Forest edges and clearings (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: canopy. Diet: aerial insects. Origin: Native to Southeast Asia, New Guinea, and Australia.
Fun fact: Drongos are acrobatic fliers and often mimic calls to distract other birds.
Superb Bird-of-Paradise (Lophorina superba)Least Concern
Where: Lowland rainforests and forest edges (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe). Habitat: understory. Diet: insects and fruit. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Males transform their appearance during display to a jet-black oval with shimmering blue-green breast shield.
Twelve-wired Bird-of-Paradise (Seleucidis melanoleucus)Near Threatened
Where: Lowland and hill rainforests (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: vine tangles and canopy. Diet: fruit and insects. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Male has twelve wire-like flank feathers used to display to females.
Victoria Crowned Pigeon (Goura victoria)Vulnerable
Where: Lowland forests and swampy areas in parts of PNG (provinces such as Sandaun, East Sepik, Madang). Habitat: canopy and forest floor. Diet: fruit and seeds. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: One of the largest pigeons, with an ornate lacy crest.
Wahnes' Parotia (Parotia wahnesi)Vulnerable
Where: Mid-montane uplands (provinces such as Eastern Highlands, Simbu). Habitat: understory display arenas. Diet: fruit and arthropods. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Spectacular male displays include wheeling dances with iridescent flank plating.
White-bibbed Fruit Dove (Ptilinopus rivoli)Least Concern
Where: Lowland and hill forests (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: canopy. Diet: fruit—especially figs. Origin: Native to New Guinea and surrounding islands.
Fun fact: Often remains high in the canopy and is more often heard than seen.
Wompoo Fruit Dove (Ptilinopus magnificus)Least Concern
Where: Lowland and hill rainforests (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: canopy. Diet: large fruits. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Named 'Wompoo' for its low, mournful call.
Yellow-billed Kingfisher (Syma torotoro)Least Concern
Where: Lowland and coastal forests (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: riparian and woodland edges. Diet: fish, insects and small prey. Origin: Native to New Guinea and surrounding islands.
Fun fact: Its bright bill contrasts strongly with its plumage, making it distinctive.
Yellow-faced Myna (Mino dumontii)Least Concern
Where: Villages, gardens and forest edges (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: open woodland and human-altered areas. Diet: omnivore: fruits and insects. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Mynas are social and often mimic other birds and human sounds.
Variable Goshawk (Accipiter hiogaster)Least Concern
Where: Forest edges, plantations and open woodland (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: edge habitats. Diet: small mammals, birds, and reptiles. Origin: Native to Southeast Asia and New Guinea.
Fun fact: A versatile raptor that adapts to a wide range of habitats including human-altered landscapes.
Papuan Frogmouth (Podargus papuensis)Least Concern
Where: Lowland forests and secondary growth (provinces such as Central, Milne Bay, Oro, Morobe, Madang). Habitat: understory and perches. Diet: insects. Origin: Endemic to New Guinea.
Fun fact: Excellent camouflage — they sit motionless on branches resembling broken stumps.
Banded Kingfisher (Lacedo pulchella)Near Threatened
Where: Interior lowland forests (provinces such as Central, Gulf, Western). Habitat: mid-levels of forest. Diet: insects and small vertebrates. Origin: Native to Southeast Asia and New Guinea.
Fun fact: Distinctive banded plumage and a loud, ringing call make it noticeable despite shy habits.
